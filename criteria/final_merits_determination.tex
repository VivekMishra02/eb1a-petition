\subsection{Legal Standard and Nature of the Field}
\label{finalmerits:standard}

The regulatory criteria establish the evidentiary baseline under Step One of the \textit{Kazarian} framework. Once the petitioner demonstrates satisfaction of at least three criteria (here, satisfying six criteria), the adjudicator evaluates the totality of the evidence in a Final Merits Determination (Step Two) to determine whether the petitioner has demonstrated sustained national or international acclaim and that his achievements have been recognized in the field of expertise, placing him among that small percentage at the very top of the field. \textit{Kazarian v. USCIS}, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010); USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 6, Part F, Ch. 2.

\dr's field is \textbf{Applied Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Critical Infrastructure Systems}. In this applied discipline, standing at the top of the field is measured not by academic citation volume alone, but by \textbf{institutional reliance and real-world deployment}: whether federal government agencies, state transportation departments, systemically important financial institutions, and leading academic institutions adopt, rely upon, and integrate the petitioner's methodologies and production systems.

In this field, extraordinary ability is demonstrated through: (1) permanent federal archival of technical methodologies deemed authoritative for long-term reference, (2) cross-state adoption of machine learning frameworks by state transportation departments, (3) hands-on engineering leadership of mission-critical production systems serving tens of millions of consumers with 99.9\% reliability, and (4) peer selection to evaluate cutting-edge research at the world's most prestigious AI/ML conferences. These independent validations demonstrate that \drs contributions have moved beyond theoretical inquiry to become infrastructure upon which critical institutions rely.
